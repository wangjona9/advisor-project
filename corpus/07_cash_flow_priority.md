When a household has a recurring monthly surplus, the next use of cash depends on liquidity needs, expensive debt, and available employer retirement benefits. A useful first review is whether the household has enough readily available cash for routine shocks and near-term obligations.

High-interest revolving debt can create a large and predictable drag on household finances. A household should compare the interest cost of that debt with other uses of incremental cash. If an employer retirement plan offers a matching contribution, failing to contribute enough to receive the available match can also have a material opportunity cost.

These priorities are general educational considerations rather than a personalized allocation rule. A recommendation among competing uses of cash requires information about debt interest rates, minimum payments, emergency reserves, employer-match terms, near-term spending needs, and other constraints.
