# S5 — IRS, Retirement Topics: Vesting

- Source: Internal Revenue Service, Retirement Topics — Vesting
- Source URL: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-vesting
- Page date: No publication/update date displayed on the page
- Accessed and verified against live page: November 16, 2026
- Excerpt type: participant-facing explanation and schedule, paraphrased from the live page.

Vesting means ownership of a plan account balance. An employee's own salary deferrals are always 100% vested. Employer contributions have different vesting rules depending on plan type. SEP and SIMPLE IRA (and other IRA-based) plans require immediate 100% vesting. Qualified defined contribution plans, including 401(k) plans, may use schedules specified in the plan document. The live IRS participant page says these can range from immediate vesting, to 100% after three years of service, to a gradual schedule.

The page's illustrative schedules are:

| Years of service | Cliff schedule | Graded schedule |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0% | 0% |
| 2 | 0% | 20% |
| 3 | 100% | 40% |
| 4 | 100% | 60% |
| 5 | 100% | 80% |
| 6 | 100% | 100% |

The IRS notes that employers may count service in different ways. All employees must be fully vested by normal retirement age under the plan or when the plan terminates. For an individual's actual terms, the IRS directs participants to their employer or HR department, Summary Plan Description, or annual benefits statement.

## Grounding boundary

The table is the IRS page's illustrative cliff and graded schedule. It is not a substitute for checking the user's plan document or determining which legal schedule applies to a particular plan or contribution type. Ask the user to verify the employer's actual match vesting terms.
