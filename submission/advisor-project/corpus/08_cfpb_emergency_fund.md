# S1 — CFPB, An essential guide to building an emergency fund

- Source: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)
- Source URL: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/
- Page last modified: October 29, 2025 (as reported in the assignment source inventory)
- Accessed: November 16, 2026
- Excerpt type: concise paraphrase of the sections “What is an emergency fund?”, “Why do I need it?”, and “How much do I need in it?”

An emergency fund is cash set aside for unplanned expenses or financial emergencies, such as car or home repairs, medical bills, or loss of income. It can cover large or small bills outside routine monthly spending. A financial shock can set someone back; if the cost becomes debt, it may have a lasting effect. People with little savings may rely on credit or loans, which can be harder to pay off, or withdraw from other savings such as retirement funds.

The amount to keep depends on the person's situation. Past unexpected expenses and their costs can help someone choose a savings goal. Even a small amount set aside can provide some financial security when saving is difficult. The guide does not prescribe a universal number of months of expenses.

## Grounding boundary

This source supports the purpose of emergency savings and the fact that an appropriate amount depends on the individual's circumstances. It does not provide a fixed months-of-expenses target or an ordering rule among savings, debt repayment, and an employer match.
